# Research and planning

## Evidence ledger

Maintain a working table or JSON records with claim, URL, checked_on, evidence type, applicable dates/party, caveat and where used. Keep extensive working notes outside the reader-facing report. Readers need concise citations and consequential limitations, not your research diary.

| Evidence | What it can support | What it cannot prove |
|---|---|---|
| Official venue page | Published opening times, history, admission | Future uninterrupted access |
| Date-specific booking result | Displayed rate for the selected basket | A held room or guaranteed future rate |
| Recent visitor accounts | Repeated patterns, atmosphere, queue anecdotes | Universal experience or binding policy |
| Property photos | Visible room style and arrangement | Wi-Fi speed, noise or exact assigned unit |
| Routing result | Modelled journey for a mode/time | Guaranteed arrival time |

A claim can be useful while uncertain. State the uncertainty precisely and keep a workable alternative. If a source blocks access, use an available credible source and say what could not be checked; do not fabricate a quote or bypass access controls.

## Feasibility

Build a calendar before writing daily prose. Keep booked segments and private commitments fixed. Count accommodation nights as checkout minus check-in. Account for midnight events, early flights, luggage, check-in gaps and separate travellers' departures. Use local timezone names, not a silently assumed offset.

Check both the event calendar and the actual ticket's access days. Confirm museum closed days, last-entry times and whether a proposed section/gallery is accessible. Avoid promising specific wildlife sightings or a quiet attraction at a universally busy period.

Use actual neighbourhoods and road/transit routes. Distinguish walking, driving and transit times; note estimates and traffic-sensitive legs. Offer a driver when continuity or coverage justifies one, with hire hours, distance allowance, waiting, parking and toll basis if known. Do not present ride-hailing estimates as fixed fares.

## Accommodation selection

Prioritise the user's actual aesthetic and location preferences. Inspect a listing's full relevant gallery rather than only its hero image. Verify listing/room identity, number of bedrooms and real beds, shared versus private spaces, stairs/lift only when relevant, ratings with review count and recent patterns. Record minimum-stay rules and total price including mandatory fees. A host's Wi-Fi claim remains a claim unless measured evidence is available; suggest how to confirm if work depends on it.

Use the listing's approximate neighbourhood for maps when the exact address is withheld. Never invent precise apartment pins. Do not conflate a hotel's best suite photos with the quoted basic room.

## Budgets

Use a common currency and exchange-rate date; separate exchange conversion from card/ATM costs. Compare identical dates, travellers, occupancy and included categories. Include tickets, transfers, food, local transport, fees and contingency where relevant. Mark excluded and already-paid items clearly. Refundable deposits affect cash flow, not necessarily final expense.

If a lower budget requires fewer nights, different rooms or omitted tickets, show that trade-off. Do not call a target allocation a bookable package. Do not count an unconfirmed discount, split a three-person room rate across four, or mix per-night and whole-stay amounts.

## Freshness sweep

Before final export search for relative language and time-sensitive claims: next, current, now, available, sold out, sale, early bird, book by, cancellation, visa, timetable. Recheck consequential claims close to delivery. Replace expired promises with historical context or unresolved status. Recheck passport-dependent entry requirements only when in scope, from official sources; never infer citizenship from departure city.
